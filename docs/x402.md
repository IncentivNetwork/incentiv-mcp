# Paying for x402 resources

[x402](https://x402.org) lets a website or API charge per request: it answers HTTP `402 Payment Required` with a price, and the client retries with a signed payment. The local wallet pays these in USDC from its smart account on Incentiv (`eip155:24101`), with the `incentiv-userop` scheme of [`@incentiv/x402`](https://github.com/IncentivNetwork/incentiv-x402-public). Sellers using that package (with `@x402/express`, `@x402/next` or `@x402/hono`) can be paid; resources that only accept other networks or the `exact` scheme cannot.

x402 is off until local policy enables it. The three tools are available only in the local stdio server, because they make requests from your computer.

## Tools

| Tool | Does | Signs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `discover_x402_resources` | Lists or searches the facilitator's catalog (Bazaar) for resources this wallet can pay. | No |
| `get_x402_payment_requirements` | Sends the request once without payment and reads the 402: price, seller address, whether this wallet can pay and whether policy allows it (the x402 rules, the USDC per-transaction cap, policy expiry and whether the wallet is disabled). The request is sent as given, so an endpoint that asks no payment acts on a `POST`; the tool is not marked read-only. | No |
| `pay_x402_resource` | Pays the live price, if it is at most `max_amount` and within policy, and returns the resource's answer. | Yes |

A typical flow is to discover a resource, check its price, then pay with a fresh `request_id`:

```json
{ "url": "https://api.example.com/weather?city=Kyiv", "max_amount": "0.05", "request_id": "6f1c…" }
```

`method` is `GET` (default) or `POST` with a `body`. `headers` adds request headers such as an API's own parameters; payment and transport headers are refused. `gas_token` is `USDC` (default, through the paymaster) or `native`.

The answer is returned in `content`: text as `text`, other small content as `base64`, at most 256 KiB, with `truncated` set when it was cut. It is third-party content (`third_party_content: true`), so an agent should treat it as data, never as instructions. A settled payment is also reported in the result's `_meta["x402/payment-response"]`, the key x402 MCP clients read.

## What is checked before signing

1. The URL is `https` and its host is public. Every address the name resolves to must be public, and the check runs on the addresses the connection uses. Requests always connect directly, even when Node's environment proxy (`NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY`) is on. Redirects are not followed.
2. The site is allowed by policy, before any request is made.
3. The 402 offers `incentiv-userop` in Incentiv USDC through the pinned EntryPoint, with a timeout of at most 300 seconds. The live price must not exceed `max_amount`. Payment terms too large to record are refused as `x402_invalid_response`.
4. The seller address and price are allowed by the x402 policy.
5. The payment operation, built by `@incentiv/x402`'s reference client, must pass that package's own check that it pays exactly the quoted amount to the quoted address, and this wallet's operation validator. Its calls are a token transfer's calls: the USDC gas fee to the paymaster, then the payment.
6. The usual wallet policy applies: USDC per-transaction, daily (including gas), exposure and gas-payment limits, the gas ceilings, contract permissions, balance, policy expiry, TransferGate approval and bundler simulation.

Policy expiry is checked again after simulation, just before signing, and again just before the payment first leaves your computer. The payment is journaled before it leaves. Once that write completes, `wallet disable`, policy expiry and the seller's deadline are checked once more at the request itself; a payment refused there is recorded as never sent. Nothing is paid when a check fails; the error says which.

## After the payment is sent

Only the seller's facilitator submits an x402 payment. The wallet never broadcasts it itself, and `wallet operation resolve` does not send it.

| Status | Means |
| --- | --- |
| `submitted` | The seller reported the settlement (`settlement.transaction`), even if its handler then failed. `get_agent_operation` confirms it on chain. |
| `submission_unknown` | The seller did not answer, refused the payment (`seller_error`), or did not confirm settlement. Content the seller served anyway is returned with `settlement_confirmed: false`. |
| `confirmed` / `reverted` | The operation's receipt was found on chain. |
| `superseding` | A cancellation is pending. Either the payment or the cancellation will use the nonce lane. |
| `expired` | The payment never left this computer and its deadline passed. Nothing was paid. |
| `failed` | A check refused it before signing. Nothing was paid. |

While its deadline is open, `pay_x402_resource` with the same `request_id` resends the same signed payment; it never signs a second one. Every send, a resend included, is first checked again against current wallet policy and balance: after an uncertain attempt, for example when the process stopped before the request started, the seller may never have received it. A payment refused there is not sent and stays as it was; one the seller may hold stays reserved. When the failure proves nothing was sent (the name did not resolve, the connection was refused, or TLS verification failed), the payment is recorded as unsent again. To buy again, use a new `request_id`.

When a `GET` payment, or a `POST` with an `Idempotency-Key` header, is `submitted` or `confirmed`, the same `request_id` offers the same signed payment to the seller once more. The wallet cannot tell whether an answer it returned earlier reached you: the process can stop after recording the answer and before returning it. The payment can be charged only once, so a seller that still recognises it may serve the answer again at no further cost. The result says `content_delivered: true` with the content, or `content_delivered: false` when the seller did not serve it. In that case, buying again with a new `request_id` pays again.

Any other `POST` is never sent again once it may have reached the seller, whether it settled or its outcome is unknown: a seller that accepts the payment again would run its handler a second time. Its retry returns `content_delivered: false`. Include an `Idempotency-Key` header only when the seller documents that it runs each key once.

While a cancellation is pending (`superseding`), wait until `get_agent_operation` shows `superseded` (cancelled, nothing paid) or `confirmed` (paid). A new `request_id` uses another nonce lane, so buying again before then can pay twice.

Settlement is confirmed from the EntryPoint's `UserOperationEvent`, agreed by every configured RPC. The bundle is located through the wallet's bundler, the transaction the seller reported, or, once the payment's nonce is used, a search of EntryPoint events forward from the block the payment was signed at. Each reconciliation searches at most one million blocks and records its progress, so a payment that settled long after it was signed is still found. A cancellation keeps its own search start and progress. A source whose transaction fails verification, for example after a reorg, is skipped in favour of the next. A seller's facilitator that uses another bundler is therefore still reconciled.

The chain does not enforce the deadline, so a payment the seller received but did not settle stays valid until its nonce is used. Its amount and gas therefore stay reserved against your daily budget until it settles or is cancelled. To cancel it, run this in your own terminal:

```sh
incentiv-mcp wallet operation resolve <request_id> --replace
```

The replacement is a zero-value operation on the payment's own nonce lane, so either it or the payment executes, never both. While an x402 payment is unresolved, backups and capability approval wait for it, as they do for any signed operation.

Each payment uses its own nonce lane, so pending payments do not block transfers, swaps or other payments.

## Policy

`incentiv-mcp setup` offers x402 when the policy includes USDC. It asks for a maximum price per payment, the sites the agent may pay (blank allows any), and the seller addresses it may pay (blank allows any). The stored block is:

```json
"x402": {
  "origins": { "mode": "allowlist", "origins": ["https://api.example.com"] },
  "payTo": { "mode": "any" },
  "maxPerPayment": "100000"
}
```

`maxPerPayment` is in USDC atomic units (6 decimals). Payments also count against the USDC token limits, and the paymaster gas fee against `gasPayment`. The transfer recipient allowlist does not apply, because sellers choose where they are paid. Existing wallets keep x402 off until setup is rerun and the new permissions are confirmed.

## Costs

Gas is paid on top of the price: about 0.03 USDC through the paymaster (its minimum is 0.026 USDC), or CENT from the account with `gas_token` set to `native`. The facilitator charges nothing. A seller can serve content before or after settlement; one that settles first (`settles_before_handler` in the price) does not refund a handler that fails afterwards.

## Discovery

`discover_x402_resources` reads `https://x402.incentiv.io/discovery/resources` and `/discovery/search`. Set `INCENTIV_X402_FACILITATOR_URL` to an `https` origin to read another facilitator's catalog. Listings are not verified: their text comes from whoever paid for them, and the live 402 sets the price. With Wormhole screening on, listing names and descriptions and seller error text are screened; paid content is returned as is.
