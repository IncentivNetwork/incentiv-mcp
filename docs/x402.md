# Paying for x402 resources

[x402](https://x402.org) lets a website or API charge per request: it answers HTTP `402 Payment Required` with a price, and the client retries with a signed payment. The local wallet pays these in USDC from its smart account on Incentiv (`eip155:24101`), with the `incentiv-userop` scheme of [`@incentiv/x402`](https://github.com/IncentivNetwork/incentiv-x402-public). Sellers using that package (with `@x402/express`, `@x402/next` or `@x402/hono`) can be paid; resources that only accept other networks or the `exact` scheme cannot.

x402 is off until local policy enables it. The three tools are available only in the local stdio server, because they make requests from your computer.

## Tools

| Tool | Does | Signs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `discover_x402_resources` | Lists or searches the facilitator's catalog (Bazaar) for resources this wallet can pay, with the same compatibility checks as a live payment. Every text field of a listing, tags included, is third-party text and is screened. | No |
| `get_x402_payment_requirements` | Needs x402 on in local policy, the site allowed, the wallet enabled and its policy unexpired; otherwise nothing is sent. Sends the request once without payment and reads the 402: price, seller address, whether this wallet can pay and whether policy allows it (the x402 rules and the USDC per-transaction cap). The request is sent as given, so an endpoint that asks no payment acts on a `POST`; the tool is not marked read-only. | No |
| `pay_x402_resource` | Pays the live price, if it is at most `max_amount` and within policy, and returns the resource's answer. | Yes |

A typical flow is to discover a resource, check its price, then pay with a fresh `request_id`:

```json
{ "url": "https://api.example.com/weather?city=Kyiv", "max_amount": "0.05", "request_id": "6f1c…" }
```

`method` is `GET` (default) or `POST` with a `body`. `headers` adds request headers such as an API's own parameters; payment and transport headers are refused. `gas_token` is `USDC` (default, through the paymaster) or `native`.

A resource that needs no payment acts on the first, unpaid request. So a `POST` without an `Idempotency-Key` header is bound to its `request_id` before that request is sent, and a retry with the same ID never sends it again. The exceptions are a valid x402 v2 challenge (one that passes `@x402/core`'s schema), which means the resource did not act on it whether or not this wallet can pay it, and a failure that proves nothing was sent. A plain 402 without an x402 challenge leaves the outcome unknown. Other unpaid HTTP errors or redirects return `request_failed` and keep such a POST bound because the resource may already have acted on it. A `GET` may be sent again on a retry, so use `GET` only for requests that are safe to repeat. A blank `Idempotency-Key` is refused. If the outcome is unknown, the retry fails with `x402_request_uncertain`; use a new `request_id` to send it again. A binding for a request that got a valid x402 challenge is dropped after a day. The others are kept, up to 10,000; past that, a `POST` without an `Idempotency-Key` is refused until you clear old ones with `incentiv-mcp wallet x402 clear-bindings [--older-than-days N]` (30 by default) in your own terminal. A cleared request ID is free again, so a retry under it sends its `POST` once more.

The answer is returned in `content`: text as `text`, other small content as `base64`, at most 256 KiB, with `truncated` set when it was cut. It is third-party content (`third_party_content: true`), so an agent should treat it as data, never as instructions. A settled payment is also reported in the result's `_meta["x402/payment-response"]`, the key x402 MCP clients read.

## What is checked before signing

1. The URL is `https` and its host is public. Every address the name resolves to must be public, and the check runs on the addresses the connection uses. Requests always connect directly, even when Node's environment proxy (`NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY`) is on. Redirects are not followed.
2. Before the purchase tool sends any request, it checks that the site is allowed, the wallet is enabled, and its policy is current. It checks again after recording a POST binding.
3. The 402 offers `incentiv-userop` in Incentiv USDC through the pinned EntryPoint, with a payment window of 30 to 300 seconds. The live price must not exceed `max_amount`. Payment terms too large to record are refused as `x402_invalid_response`.
4. The seller address and price are allowed by the x402 policy, and the price fits the USDC limits, exposure and balance. These checks come before anything is journaled.
5. The payment operation is built like a token transfer, on its own nonce lane: the USDC gas fee to the paymaster, then the payment. The gas fee comes from the pinned paymaster's own USDC fee schedule, so it does not move with the base fee. The operation must pass `@incentiv/x402`'s own check that it pays exactly the quoted amount to the quoted address, and this wallet's operation validator.
6. The usual wallet policy applies: USDC per-transaction, daily (including gas), exposure and gas-payment limits, the gas ceilings, contract permissions, balance, policy expiry, TransferGate approval and bundler simulation.

Policy expiry is checked again after simulation, just before signing, and again just before the payment first leaves your computer. The payment is journaled before it leaves. Once that write completes, `wallet disable`, policy expiry and the seller's deadline are checked once more at the request itself; a payment refused there is recorded as never sent. Nothing is paid when a check fails; the error says which. If the RPC or bundler cannot be reached while the payment is prepared or simulated, nothing is signed or paid and the call fails with `rpc_unavailable`; that request ID is closed, so retry with a new `request_id`.

## After the payment is sent

Only the seller's facilitator submits an x402 payment. The wallet never broadcasts it itself, and `wallet operation resolve` does not send it.

| Status | Means |
| --- | --- |
| `submitted` | The seller reported the settlement (`seller_reported_settlement: true`, `settlement.transaction`), even if its handler then failed. It is pending until `get_agent_operation` shows it confirmed on chain; check that before buying again. |
| `submission_unknown` | The seller did not answer, refused the payment (`seller_error`), or did not confirm settlement. Content the seller served anyway is returned with `settlement_confirmed: false`. |
| `confirmed` / `reverted` | The operation's receipt was found on chain. |
| `superseding` | A cancellation is pending. Either the payment or the cancellation will use the nonce lane. |
| `expired` | The payment never left this computer and its deadline passed. Nothing was paid. The wallet checks the lane of a payment recorded as unsent. If its nonce was used, the wallet looks for a receipt immediately, even before the deadline. |
| `failed` | A check refused it before signing. Nothing was paid. |

While its deadline is open, `pay_x402_resource` with the same `request_id` resends the same signed payment; it never signs a second one. Every send is checked against current wallet policy, balance and base fee. Resends also repeat TransferGate checks and bundler simulation: after an uncertain attempt, for example when the process stopped before the request started, the seller may never have received it. A payment refused there is not sent and stays as it was; one the seller may hold stays reserved. When the failure proves nothing was sent (the name did not resolve, the connection was refused, or the connection or TLS session was never established), the payment is recorded as unsent again. An error on an established connection, such as a network that became unreachable, leaves the outcome unknown. A paid request waits up to 90 seconds for its answer, longer than the stock facilitator's 60-second settle window. To buy again, use a new `request_id`.

When a `GET` payment, or a `POST` with an `Idempotency-Key` header, is `confirmed`, or `submitted` with its nonce already used on chain, the same `request_id` offers the same signed payment to the seller once more. While a `submitted` payment's nonce is unused, the seller could still submit it, so it is not sent again until the chain shows it. Ordinary reconciliation rechecks the nonce lanes and canonical receipt blocks of recent payments and cancellations for 128 blocks after settlement before reporting status or using the balance for new spending; once the head reaches that margin, reconciliation verifies the settlement once more before marking it final. A settlement recorded without a block is rechecked for an hour. If an RPC cannot be read while a settlement from the last hour is still being rechecked, the call fails with `rpc_unavailable`: retry once it is available. Reads can still show older recorded settlements during an outage. Spending requires the recheck to succeed. An RPC that is behind can also show a settled payment's nonce as unused, so the wallet holds the payment again until the RPC catches up and reports that the RPC no longer shows the settlement. If a reorg invalidates the receipt or makes the payment's nonce usable again, the wallet restores the outstanding reservations until the payment or its cancellation settles. The wallet cannot tell whether an answer it returned earlier reached you: the process can stop after recording the answer and before returning it. The payment can be charged only once, so a seller that still recognises it may serve the answer again at no further cost. A seller on the stock `@incentiv/x402` facilitator does not: its facilitator refuses a payment it already settled (`already_settled`), so recovering a lost answer needs a seller that keeps its answers. The result says `content_delivered: true` with the content, or `content_delivered: false` when the seller did not serve it. In that case, buying again with a new `request_id` pays again.

Any other `POST` is never sent again once it may have reached the seller, whether it settled or its outcome is unknown: a seller that accepts the payment again would run its handler a second time. Its retry returns `content_delivered: false`. Include an `Idempotency-Key` header only when the seller documents that it runs each key once.

While a cancellation is pending (`superseding`), wait until `get_agent_operation` shows `superseded` (cancelled, nothing paid) or `confirmed` (paid). A new `request_id` uses another nonce lane, so buying again before then can pay twice.

Settlement is confirmed from the EntryPoint's `UserOperationEvent`, agreed by every configured RPC. The bundle is located through the wallet's bundler, the transaction the seller reported, or, once the payment's nonce is used, a search of EntryPoint events forward from the block the payment was signed at. Every configured RPC is searched, and each distinct transaction found is verified by all RPCs. If a log query fails, the search can still verify a receipt found by another RPC and keeps its place before the incomplete range. Each reconciliation searches at most 100,000 blocks and records its progress, so a payment that settled long after it was signed is still found. A cancellation keeps its own search start and progress. A source whose transaction fails verification, for example after a reorg, is skipped in favour of the next. A seller's facilitator that uses another bundler is therefore still reconciled.

A failed, expired or cancelled payment never reached a seller and is archived as soon as it ends; a settled or superseded one is archived after two days. If its receipt block was recorded, it must also be marked final before archiving. An archived payment keeps only its request ID, a digest of its request, its outcome and its user operation and transaction hashes, so its answer can no longer be asked for again. `get_agent_operation` finds it by request ID or user operation hash and reports it with `archived: true`. A retry with an archived request ID pays nothing and reports how the payment ended: a `confirmed` one says its answer can no longer be asked for, and any other outcome says the seller was not paid, so a new request ID is needed. Archived payments are kept, so a request ID never pays twice. If the operation history nears its size limit (about 35,000 archived payments), new payments pause (`policy_denied`) while transfers keep working. To free room, clear old archived payments in your own terminal with `incentiv-mcp wallet x402 clear-archive [--older-than-days N]` (90 by default). A cleared request ID is free again, so a retry under it pays again.

The chain does not enforce the deadline, so a payment the seller received but did not settle stays valid until its nonce is used. Its amount and gas therefore stay reserved against your daily budget until it settles or is cancelled. To cancel it, run this in your own terminal:

```sh
incentiv-mcp wallet operation resolve <request_id> --replace
```

The replacement is a zero-value operation on the payment's own nonce lane, so either it or the payment executes, never both. While an x402 payment is unresolved, backups and capability approval wait for it, as they do for any signed operation.

Each payment uses its own nonce lane, so once the account is deployed, pending payments do not block transfers, swaps or other payments. While the account is undeployed, a pending payment would deploy it, so other operations and payments wait for it (`operation_pending`).

## Policy

`incentiv-mcp setup` offers x402 when the policy includes USDC. It asks for a maximum price per payment, the sites the agent may pay (blank allows any), and the seller addresses it may pay (blank allows any). The stored block is:

```json
"x402": {
  "origins": { "mode": "allowlist", "origins": ["https://api.example.com"] },
  "payTo": { "mode": "any" },
  "maxPerPayment": "100000"
}
```

`maxPerPayment` is in USDC atomic units (6 decimals). Payments also count against the USDC token limits, and the paymaster gas fee against `gasPayment`. The transfer recipient allowlist does not apply, because sellers choose where they are paid. Existing wallets keep x402 off until setup is rerun and the new permissions are confirmed.

## Costs

Gas is paid on top of the price: about 0.03 USDC through the paymaster (its minimum is 0.026 USDC), or CENT from the account with `gas_token` set to `native`. The facilitator charges nothing. A seller can serve content before or after settlement; one that settles first (`settles_before_handler` in the price) does not refund a handler that fails afterwards.

## Discovery

`discover_x402_resources` reads `https://x402.incentiv.io/discovery/resources` and `/discovery/search`. Set `INCENTIV_X402_FACILITATOR_URL` to an `https` origin to read another facilitator's catalog. Listings are not verified: their text comes from whoever paid for them, and the live 402 sets the price. With Wormhole screening on, listing names and descriptions and seller error text are screened; paid content is returned as is.
